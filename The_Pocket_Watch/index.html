The Pocket Watch


Some facts about the pocket watch

  • Pocket watches once represented personal time zones — Before standardized time, people set their pocket watches to local noon using the sun, so two nearby towns could have slightly different times!
  • Railroads made them famous — Pocket watches became essential after the 1891 Kipton train crash in the US, which happened due to a conductor’s inaccurate watch. This led to the creation of strict “railroad-grade” timepieces.
  • They weren’t always carried in pockets — The earliest pocket watches (1500s) were worn as pendants around the neck because they were too bulky for pockets.
  • They had no glass at first — Early pocket watches used metal lids instead of glass because glass was too fragile. Transparent watch crystals only became common in the 17th century.
